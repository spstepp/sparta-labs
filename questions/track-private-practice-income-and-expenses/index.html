How do therapists track income and expenses in private practice?

Use one business bank account and keep two logs: money in and money out. Log income when it's actually received, with the source (insurance, client copay or self-pay, other). Give every expense a category that matches your tax return. Once a month, look at profit for the month and check the logs against your bank statement. That's most of the bookkeeping a solo practice needs between CPA visits.

How to set it up

  1. Keep practice money in its own account. One business checking account and one card for practice spending make every later step easier. Moving money to your personal account is a transfer or owner draw, not an expense.
  2. Log income on the date it's received, with the source and payment method. Count each insurance payment once. If you track claims somewhere, record the payment there or here, not both.
  3. Give every expense a category. If you file a Schedule C, use categories that line up with its lines, such as rent, insurance, supplies, software, continuing education and supervision. Ask your CPA which categories fit your situation.
  4. Mark anything that isn't a business expense instead of leaving it out, so the log still matches the bank.
  5. Check one monthly profit number: income received minus business expenses. Watching it each month shows a slow month or a rising cost early.
  6. Reconcile monthly. Mark each item as it clears on the bank statement and compare the balance. A gap usually means a missing entry, a duplicate, or a deposit still in transit.
  7. Keep receipts and statements where you can find them. Your CPA can tell you how long to keep them.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax or accounting advice. Which expenses are deductible, how they're categorized and which accounting method you use depend on your situation, so check with a CPA or tax professional.

Common follow-up questions

Do I need bookkeeping software, or is a spreadsheet enough?

For a solo practice with one business account, a spreadsheet is often enough if you keep it up every month. If you have staff, several accounts or a CPA who wants a specific system, software may fit better. Ask your CPA what they need from you.

Should I count income when I bill or when I'm paid?

Many solo practices use the cash method, which counts income when it's received. Your CPA can confirm which method you use.

How often should I update it?

Weekly is easiest, since there's less to remember. At minimum, catch up and reconcile once a month after your bank statement arrives.
