How do I record insurance payments from an EOB or ERA without counting them twice?

Record an insurance payment in one place only: on the claim it pays. When the EOB or ERA comes in, find the claim by client ID and date of service, enter what the payer actually paid and the date, and mark it paid. Then compare the payment with what you expected for that code. If it's short, the claim isn't really done yet. The deposit itself may bundle several claims, so match the payer's total to your bank, not each claim.

How to set it up

  1. Keep one row per claim with date of service, client ID (never a name), payer, CPT code, billed amount and the amount you expect under your contract.
  2. Open the EOB or ERA and find each claim on it. One remittance often covers several clients and dates, so work through it line by line.
  3. Enter the paid amount and the date paid on the claim row, and change its status to Paid. Don't also type the payment as income somewhere else, or it gets counted twice.
  4. Check paid against expected. If the payer paid less than your contracted rate, note why from the adjustment codes on the EOB and follow up, or mark it written off if you decide to let it go.
  5. Log the client's share separately. A copay, coinsurance or deductible amount on the EOB is what the client owes you, not insurance income.
  6. Match the payer's deposit to your bank. Add up the claims on the remittance and check that total against the deposit, then mark each claim cleared.

This is record-keeping guidance, not billing or tax advice. Payer rules and adjustment codes vary, so check your contracts and ask your biller or CPA when something doesn't add up.

Common follow-up questions

What's the difference between an EOB and an ERA?

They carry the same information. An EOB is the explanation of benefits, often on paper or as a PDF. An ERA is the electronic version that comes with an electronic payment. Either one tells you what was paid on each claim.

What if one deposit pays claims for several clients?

That's normal. Post each claim from the remittance, then check that the claims add up to the deposit amount on your bank statement.

Where does the client's copay go?

On the client side, as money the client owes or paid you. It's not part of the insurance payment, even though it shows on the EOB.
