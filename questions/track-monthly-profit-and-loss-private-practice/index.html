How do therapists make a monthly profit and loss statement in private practice?

A monthly profit and loss lists the money the practice actually received that month, the business expenses for that month, and the difference (net profit). Split income by source (insurance payments, client payments, other) and list expenses by category. Transfers to a personal or tax savings account stay off the statement, because they aren't income or expenses. Review the month you just closed, and keep a 12-month view so one unusual month doesn't hide the year.

How to put one together

  1. Pick the month you just closed. A P&L for a month that's still open will change as late deposits and expenses arrive.
  2. Add income by the date it was received, not the date you billed. Keep insurance payments, client payments (copays, self-pay, fees) and other income (workshops, speaking) on separate lines so you can see where the money came from.
  3. Count each insurance payment once. If you also keep a claims log, treat a paid claim as income on the date it was paid, and don't type the same deposit into the income log again.
  4. List only business expenses, grouped by category (rent, software, supplies, insurance, continuing education and so on). If you file a Schedule C, use categories that line up with its lines. Ask your CPA which expenses belong.
  5. Leave out money you moved yourself, such as an owner draw or a transfer to a tax savings account. Those change the bank balance, but they aren't income or expenses, so they don't belong on the P&L.
  6. Subtract expenses from income to get net profit for the month. Looking at each line as a share of income helps you see whether one category is growing.
  7. Keep a 12-month summary next to the month you just reviewed, so a slow month or a large one-time expense has context.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax or accounting advice. Which expenses are deductible, how they're categorized and which accounting method you use depend on your situation, so check with a CPA or tax professional.

Common follow-up questions

Is a P&L the same as a bank reconciliation?

No. A P&L shows income and expenses for a period and the profit left. A reconciliation checks that your records match a bank statement. You want both, and they answer different questions.

Should owner draws show on the P&L?

No. Moving money from the practice account to your personal account isn't an expense. Record it separately so the bank still ties out, but leave it off the profit and loss.

Do unpaid claims count as income on a monthly P&L?

Not if you count income when it's received. An unpaid claim is still outstanding; it belongs on your claims follow-up list until it's paid or written off.
