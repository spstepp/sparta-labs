How do I track RBT direct service hours each month?

Log every session with the date, client ID, CPT code, units, the RBT's staff code and a status. Count only completed sessions, convert units to hours the way your payer does, and total them by RBT for each calendar month. That one number feeds the supervision check and should match what was billed. Keep travel, documentation and other non-client time in your timesheet or payroll system, not in this count.

How to set it up

  1. Give every RBT a staff code and use it on each session row instead of a name.
  2. Log one row per session with date, client ID, CPT code, units and provider. Add sessions when they're scheduled, then change the status to Completed or Cancelled.
  3. Convert units to hours the same way every time. With 15-minute units, 4 units is 1 hour. If a payer rounds differently, follow the payer.
  4. Total completed hours per RBT for each calendar month. Scheduled sessions haven't happened yet and cancelled ones didn't happen, so neither belongs in the total.
  5. Check the total partway through the month. Supervision you owe an RBT goes up with their hours, and it's easier to add supervision in week two than after the month closes.
  6. Compare the session log with timesheets and billing now and then. If an RBT's hours don't match what was billed, find out why before payroll or a payer audit does.

What counts as service hours for supervision is set by the BACB, and your employer or payer may have their own rules for timesheets and billing. Check the current RBT Handbook and Supervision Checklist and your organization's policies.

Common follow-up questions

Do cancelled sessions count toward an RBT's hours?

Not toward direct service hours, since no service was given. If your organization pays RBTs for late cancels, track that in payroll, separately from the session count.

Should drive time and documentation go in the same log?

Keep them apart. They may matter for pay, but mixing them into session hours throws off both the supervision math and the comparison with what was billed.

How do I turn units into hours?

Multiply units by the minutes per unit and divide by 60. With 15-minute units, 6 units is 1.5 hours.
