How do I track denied insurance claims in a small practice?

Give every claim a status (submitted, paid, denied, resubmitted, written off) and, when it's denied, the reason. Then look at the denied and open claims every week, oldest first. Over a few months, the denial reasons show what to fix at the front end, like eligibility checks or missing authorizations, and the denial rate by payer shows who needs the most follow-up.

How to set it up

  1. One row per claim: date of service, client ID, payer, CPT code, billed, expected, paid, date submitted, date paid, and status.
  2. Use a fixed list of denial reasons, such as eligibility ended, missing prior authorization, timely filing, invalid code or modifier, duplicate, missing information. A fixed list lets you count them later.
  3. When you resubmit, change the status and the submit date rather than adding a second row, so the claim's history stays in one place. Note the reference number from any call.
  4. Watch days outstanding. Sort open and denied claims by age each week. Payers have filing and appeal deadlines, so old claims are the ones at risk.
  5. Write off on purpose. If a claim won't be paid, mark it written off so it stops showing as money you're owed.
  6. Review the reasons monthly. If most denials are eligibility or missing auths, a check before the first session fixes more than any follow-up will.

Timely filing and appeal deadlines differ by payer and plan. Check your payer contracts, and follow your organization's billing policies.

Common follow-up questions

Do I need a separate appeals log?

For a few appeals a month, a status and a note on the claim row is usually enough. If you file many formal appeals, a separate list with each deadline can help.

What's a reasonable denial rate?

It depends on your payers and services, so compare against your own history. A rate that's climbing, or one payer that denies far more than the others, is the thing to look into.

Our billing service tracks this. Do I need a spreadsheet?

If your biller sends you a clear denial report and aging, you may not. A spreadsheet helps if you bill yourself or want your own view of what's owed.
