How do I record card payments when the processor takes a fee?

Record what the client paid, not what landed in your bank. Say a client pays $150 by card and the processor keeps $4.65: log $150 as income and $4.65 as a card processing fee. The processor deposits the difference, often several payments at once, so match each payout to the payments and fees inside it. Logged this way, your card income lines up with the gross amount on any Form 1099-K you get, and the fees still count as a business expense.

How to set it up

  1. Log each client payment at the full amount on the date the client paid, with Card or HSA/FSA as the method and a client ID, never a name.
  2. Get the fees from your processor's monthly statement or payout report and log them as one expense a month, or one per payout if you'd rather match exactly.
  3. Match payouts to your bank. Several payments minus fees show up as one deposit. The payout report lists what's in each one.
  4. Watch the timing. A Friday payment can land on Tuesday, and a payment on the 31st can arrive next month. Date the income when the client paid and mark it cleared when the payout arrives.
  5. Log refunds and chargebacks as their own rows instead of editing the original payment. Check whether your processor gives the fee back on a refund; some don't.
  6. Compare your card income for the year with any Form 1099-K you receive. The 1099-K shows gross payments before fees and refunds, so it should sit close to your card income, not your deposits.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax advice. How you report card income and fees depends on your situation, so check with a CPA or tax professional. The IRS explains the form at Understanding your Form 1099-K.

Common follow-up questions

Can I just log the deposits as income?

It's simpler, but your income will come out lower than the gross on a 1099-K, and the fees disappear as an expense. Logging the full payment and the fee separately keeps both straight.

Do HSA and FSA card payments work the same way?

On the books, yes. They usually run through the same processor with the same kind of fee. Use HSA/FSA as the method if you want to see how many clients pay that way.

Can I pass the card fee on to clients?

Surcharge rules vary by state and by card network, and some states limit them. Check your state's rules and your processor's terms before you add one.
