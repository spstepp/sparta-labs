Practice questions

Short, practical answers to the record-keeping questions therapists, BCBAs and other private practice owners ask most: authorizations, claims, CE hours, missed-session fees and supervision. Each one explains how to do it by hand and which of our templates, if any, can do it for you.

  • How do I track how many insurance authorization visits a client has left?
    A simple way for therapists to see how many authorized visits each client has left and when the authorization ends, so re-authorizations go in on time.
  • What should a therapy insurance claims tracker include?
    The columns a private practice claims tracker needs, from date of service to denial reason, and how to use days outstanding to know which claims to chase.
  • How do I track CE credits for license renewal?
    A plain system for tracking continuing education hours, required categories and certificates for one or more licenses before renewal comes around.
  • How do ABA providers track authorization units?
    How BCBAs and ABA practices track authorized units by client and CPT code, including units used, units scheduled, units left and the pace of use.
  • How do I track no-show and late-cancel fees in private practice?
    How therapists can log missed sessions, apply the fee from their written policy, and keep track of what was collected, waived or reduced.
  • How do I track RBT supervision hours each month?
    How BCBAs can compare each RBT's supervision time with their service hours for the month, and spot a shortfall while there's still time to fix it.
  • How do therapists in private practice track quarterly estimated taxes?
    How solo therapists can set money aside for quarterly estimated taxes, track what they paid each period, and see a shortfall before the due date.
  • How do I keep track of a therapy waitlist in private practice?
    A simple way for therapists to keep a waitlist: who's waiting, what they need, when you last contacted them, and who to call first when a slot opens.
  • When should ABA providers start a reauthorization request?
    How BCBAs and ABA practices plan reauthorization requests: work back from each payer's lead time, schedule the reassessment, and watch end dates and unit pace.
  • How do I track denied insurance claims in a small practice?
    How therapists and small practices log denied claims, record the denial reason, follow up on resubmissions and see which payers deny most.
  • How do therapists track income and expenses in private practice?
    A simple bookkeeping routine for solo therapists: log income by source, sort expenses by tax category, check a monthly profit number and match it to the bank.
  • How do ABA practices track RBT and BCBA credential expiration dates?
    A simple way for ABA practices to keep every RBT and BCBA certification date in one list, with warnings early enough to finish renewal before anything lapses.
  • How do therapists keep track of what clients owe?
    A plain way for private practice therapists to track session charges, payments and unpaid balances by client, grouped by age, so nothing owed gets forgotten.
  • How do therapists keep track of sliding scale fees?
    A simple way for private practice therapists to record each sliding scale client's agreed fee, log what was charged and paid, and review reduced fees on a set schedule.
  • How do therapists reconcile their practice records with the bank statement?
    A plain monthly routine for private practice therapists to check that income, insurance payments and expenses in their records match the business bank statement.
  • How do counselors track CE hours when they're licensed in more than one state?
    A simple way for counselors and therapists with licenses in several states to track each board's CE hours, required topics and renewal dates in one place.
  • How do ABA providers track parent training (97156) units against the authorization?
    How BCBAs keep caregiver training units under CPT 97156 separate from direct therapy, see what's used and booked, and catch units going unused before the authorization ends.

These pages are general record-keeping guidance, not billing, legal or clinical advice. Payer contracts and board rules vary, so check with your payer or licensing board.
