How do therapists track business mileage for taxes?

Keep a log of every business trip with the date, where you went, why, and the miles. Note your odometer at the start and end of the year so you have total miles too. Your daily drive from home to your regular office is commuting and doesn't count. Trips from that office to a school, a client's home, a hospital or a training usually do. At the end of each month, total the business miles and log them as one car expense.

How to set it up

  1. Pick one place to log trips and use it every time: a notes app, a paper log in the glovebox or a mileage app. The IRS gives more weight to a record kept at or near the time of the trip, and a weekly log counts.
  2. Write down four things per trip: date, destination, business purpose and miles. Use a place or a client ID, never a client's name. The IRS says confidential details can be kept in a separate record.
  3. Leave out commuting. Home to your regular office and back is personal, even if you take calls on the way. If your home office is your principal place of business, the rules are different; ask your CPA.
  4. Record the odometer on January 1 and December 31. The IRS asks for total miles for the year as well as business miles.
  5. Total business miles each month and record the deduction as one expense, using the method your CPA picks: the standard mileage rate times your miles, or your actual car costs times your business-use share.
  6. Keep parking and tolls for business trips separately. The IRS lets you deduct those on top of either method.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax advice. Which trips count, which method you can use and whether your home office changes the commuting rules depend on your situation, so check with a CPA or tax professional. Current rates are on the IRS standard mileage rates page.

Common follow-up questions

Does the IRS mileage rate change during the year?

It can. For 2026 the IRS set one business rate for January through June and a higher one for July through December, so check the IRS standard mileage rates page for the dates your trips fall in.

Can I reconstruct mileage at the end of the year?

You can rebuild it from your calendar and a map, but a log kept at the time carries more weight if your return is ever examined. Start a log now and use your calendar to fill in the months you missed.

Should I use the standard mileage rate or actual expenses?

It depends on your car, how much you drive for work and what you've claimed before. The IRS has rules on when you can switch, so ask your CPA before you choose.
