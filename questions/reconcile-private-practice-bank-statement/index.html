How do therapists reconcile their practice records with the bank statement?

Once a month, when the statement arrives, mark each deposit and payment in your records that shows on the statement. Start from your opening balance, add the cleared money in, take the cleared money out, and compare the result to the statement's ending balance. If they match, you're reconciled. If not, the difference tells you how much to look for.

How to do it each month

  1. Keep one business bank account for the practice, so every deposit and payment in your records belongs to it.
  2. Log money in and out as it happens: client payments, insurance payments and expenses, each with a date and amount.
  3. When the statement arrives, mark what cleared. Tick each item in your records that appears on the statement. Leave anything still in transit, like an uncashed check, unmarked.
  4. Account for money you moved yourself, such as owner draws or transfers to a tax savings account. These aren't income or expenses, but they change the bank balance.
  5. Compare balances. Opening balance, plus cleared money in, minus cleared money out, plus or minus your own transfers, should equal the statement's ending balance.
  6. If it doesn't match, look for the usual causes: a missing entry, a payment entered twice, a typo in an amount, a bank fee you didn't record, or an item marked cleared that isn't on the statement.

This is general record-keeping guidance, not accounting or tax advice. Ask your CPA or bookkeeper how they want your records kept.

Common follow-up questions

How often should I reconcile?

Once a month, when each statement arrives, is a common routine. Small mistakes are much easier to find one month at a time.

What if I use my personal account for the practice?

Reconciling is much harder when personal and practice money mix. Many CPAs suggest a separate business account. Ask yours what fits your situation.

Do transfers to my tax savings account count as expenses?

No. Moving money between your own accounts isn't income or an expense. It only changes the bank balance, so account for it separately when you reconcile.
