How do therapists track home office expenses for taxes?

If part of your home is used regularly and only for your practice, you may be able to deduct some home costs. Measure the office and the whole home, keep the year's rent or mortgage interest, utilities, insurance and repair bills, and note whether you see clients there in person or by telehealth. The IRS has two ways to figure it: a simplified method at $5 per square foot for up to 300 square feet, and a regular method on Form 8829 that uses your office's share of actual costs. Your CPA picks one, and you log the result as one expense.

How to set it up

  1. Check the basics first. The IRS generally wants the space used regularly and only for business. A guest room that doubles as your office usually doesn't qualify. If you also rent an outside office, ask your CPA how the rules apply to you.
  2. Measure the office and the whole home in square feet and write both down with the date. The office's share of the home drives the regular method.
  3. Keep a folder of the year's home bills: rent or mortgage interest, property tax, utilities, renters or homeowners insurance and repairs. Note any repair made only to the office room, since it's treated differently from whole-home costs.
  4. Log things you buy just for the office, like a lamp or a sound machine for the door, as regular business expenses when you buy them.
  5. Enter the home office amount once your CPA works it out, or a monthly estimate if you want your profit and tax set-aside to reflect it during the year.
  6. Keep the measurements and bills with that year's records, in case you're ever asked how you got the number.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax advice. Whether your space qualifies, which method you use and how renting or owning changes the math depend on your situation, so check with a CPA or tax professional. The rules are on the IRS Home office deduction page and in Publication 587.

Common follow-up questions

Does a home office count if I only do telehealth from it?

It can, if the space is used regularly and only for your practice and meets the IRS rules. Doing telehealth from the kitchen table doesn't make the kitchen an office. Ask your CPA about your setup.

Which is better, the simplified or the regular method?

The simplified method is less paperwork. The regular method can come out higher if your home costs are high, but it needs more records. Your CPA can run both.

What if I rent my home?

Renters can use either method. Under the regular method, the office's share of your rent counts the way mortgage interest and property tax would for an owner.
