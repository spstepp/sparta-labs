How do I get ready for a CE audit from my licensing board?

A CE audit usually asks you to prove the hours you reported at renewal. Be ready by keeping one log of every course with its date, provider, hours and category, plus a saved certificate for each one. Count only courses dated inside the renewal period being audited, check the required categories as well as the total, and fix any missing certificate before the board asks.

How to prepare

  1. Read the audit notice or your board's CE rules first. Note which renewal period is being checked, what proof the board accepts and the deadline to respond.
  2. Keep one list of every course, with the date, course title, provider, hours and category. Notes spread across email and old calendars are hard to defend.
  3. Match each course to a certificate. Mark which ones you have on file. Ask the provider for a copy of any that are missing. That can take time, so start early.
  4. Count only courses dated inside the audited period. Courses from the cycle before usually don't count, unless your board allows carry-over.
  5. Check category hours, not just the total. Ethics or other required topics are a common place to come up short.
  6. If you hold more than one license, check each one separately. A course may be accepted by one board and not by another.
  7. Keep old courses and certificates after you renew. Your board may audit a period after it has ended, so don't clear the record.

Audit rules, accepted proof and how long to keep certificates vary by board and profession. This is record-keeping guidance, not legal or licensing advice. Follow your board's audit notice and its CE rules.

Common follow-up questions

How long should I keep CE certificates?

At least as long as your board says it may ask for them, which can be past your renewal date. Check your board's record-keeping rule.

What if a course provider can't send a replacement certificate?

Ask your board what other proof it accepts, such as a receipt or a provider letter. Don't count the hours until you know they'll be accepted.

Can one course count for two licenses in an audit?

Only if each board accepts it. Check each board's rules and credit the course only where it's accepted.
