Do therapists in private practice have to pay quarterly estimated taxes?

Usually, yes. Nobody withholds tax from practice income, so the IRS expects you to pay during the year. In most cases you have to make estimated payments if you expect to owe at least $1,000 in federal tax for the year after withholding and refundable credits, and your withholding won't cover enough of it. For 2026 the payments are due April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Your CPA can tell you whether this applies to you and how much to pay.

How to keep track of it

  1. Ask your CPA whether you need to pay, and how much. If you or a spouse have a paycheck, extra withholding there may cover it instead.
  2. Put the four due dates on your calendar. For 2026: April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15, 2027. A due date on a weekend or legal holiday moves to the next business day.
  3. Group income by the IRS payment periods, not calendar quarters: January to March, April to May, June to August, and September to December.
  4. Set money aside as income comes in, at the rate your CPA gives you, and log each transfer.
  5. Pay before each due date and keep the confirmation. You can pay online, by phone, through your IRS online account, or by mail with a Form 1040-ES voucher.
  6. Check your state too. If your state has an income tax, it may have its own estimated payments and dates.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax advice. The $1,000 rule, the safe-harbor percentages and the due dates come from the IRS and can change, and higher-income taxpayers have different percentages. Check IRS.gov estimated taxes and Form 1040-ES, and ask your CPA.

Common follow-up questions

What if I also have a W-2 job?

Extra withholding from that paycheck can cover tax on your practice income. The IRS says you can file a new Form W-4 with your employer to have more withheld. Ask your CPA which works better for you.

What happens if I pay too little or pay late?

The IRS may charge an underpayment penalty, even if you get a refund when you file. In general you avoid it if you owe less than $1,000 after withholding and credits, or if you paid at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's, whichever is smaller.

Do I have to make the January payment?

Not if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay the whole balance with it. That's from the 2026 Form 1040-ES instructions.
