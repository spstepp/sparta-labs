How do therapists catch insurance underpayments?

Write down what you expect the payer to pay for each claim, based on your fee schedule or contract. When the EOB or ERA arrives, enter what was actually paid next to it. If the payment is lower and the EOB doesn't explain the gap with the client's share, flag the claim and follow up with the payer. Doing this as each payment is posted is easier than finding a pattern months later.

How to set it up

  1. Keep your fee schedules where you can find them. For each payer, note the rate you expect for each CPT code you bill. If you're not sure of a rate, ask the payer or check your contract.
  2. Log each claim with the date of service, client ID, payer, CPT code, billed amount and expected amount. Billed is your full fee. Expected is what the payer should pay.
  3. Post every payment from the EOB or ERA, with the amount paid and the date paid. Don't just mark the claim paid because a deposit arrived.
  4. Compare paid with expected on every claim. A gap can be the client's copay, coinsurance or deductible. If the EOB shows that, the payer didn't underpay. Collect it from the client instead.
  5. Flag what's left over and note the reason code from the EOB. Small gaps on the same code and payer, month after month, often point to an outdated rate on the payer's side.
  6. Follow up in writing or by phone, and note the date and reference number. Ask the payer how to request a review and how long you have to do it.

Contracted rates, client cost sharing and how to dispute a payment depend on each payer and plan. This is record-keeping guidance, not billing advice. Check your contract or the payer's provider manual, and call the payer if a payment doesn't match.

Common follow-up questions

What should I enter as the expected amount?

Use what you expect the payer itself to pay. If you know the client owes a copay on that claim, leave it out of the expected amount, so a gap means the payer paid short and not that the client still owes.

Is an underpayment the same as a denial?

No. A denial means the payer didn't pay the claim. An underpayment means it paid, but less than you expected. Track both, because they need different follow-up.

Should I still post an underpaid claim as Paid?

Yes, post what actually came in on the date it came in, so your income is right. Keep the gap flagged until the payer corrects it or you decide to write it off.
