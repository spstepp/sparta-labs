How should therapists categorize business expenses for taxes?

Agree on a short list of expense categories with your CPA, usually lined up with the lines on Schedule C for a solo practice. Give every expense one category when you log it, and mark anything that isn't a business expense instead of leaving it out. Check for blanks once a month. By tax time the sorting is already done, and the profit you've been setting tax money aside from is closer to the real number.

How to set it up

  1. Agree on the category list with your CPA once. A starting list that lines up with Schedule C lines is common for solo practices, but your CPA decides where each kind of cost goes.
  2. Give every expense exactly one category when you log it, along with the date, vendor and amount. Sorting a year of card statements in April is where most mix-ups happen.
  3. Mark each expense as business or not. If something personal lands on the practice card, log it and mark it personal instead of skipping it, so your records still match the bank.
  4. Add a note to anything you're unsure about, such as a purchase used partly for the practice, and ask your CPA instead of guessing.
  5. Add a new category only when you need one, and ask your CPA which tax line it belongs on. A long list of near-duplicates is harder to total.
  6. Look for blanks once a month. An expense with no category, or no business yes/no, is the one that gets missed at tax time.
  7. Keep receipts with the record, or note where they're stored, so you can back up any line your CPA asks about.

This is record-keeping guidance, not tax or accounting advice. Which expenses are deductible and which tax line they go on depend on your situation, so check your category list with a CPA or tax professional.

Common follow-up questions

Does every expense need a category, even small ones?

Yes. Small recurring charges like software subscriptions add up over a year, and an expense with no category is easy to miss when you total things up.

What if a purchase is partly personal?

Log it and add a note. Whether a mixed-use cost counts, and how to split it, is a question for your CPA.

Can I change a category later?

Yes. If you change how you categorize a whole type of expense partway through the year, tell your CPA so the totals still make sense.
