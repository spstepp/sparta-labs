What are the RBT supervision requirements?

Under the BACB's RBT Handbook (updated October 2026), an RBT who provides behavior-analytic services must be supervised for at least 5% of those service hours each calendar month. The month has to include at least two face-to-face, real-time contacts, at least one of them individual, and the supervisor has to observe the RBT working with a client in at least one. Supervisors must be BCBA or BCaBA certified, and the RBT and supervisor keep the supervision records for at least 7 years.

The requirements, one at a time

  1. At least 5% of service hours, every calendar month. The minimum is based on the hours the RBT spent providing behavior-analytic services that month. An RBT with 120 service hours in a month needs at least 6 hours of supervision.
  2. At least two contacts a month, face to face and in real time. Phone calls and email don't count. Contacts can be in person or by video, and internet-based supervision has to comply with applicable laws.
  3. At least one contact is individual, with no other RBTs or trainees present. The other can be a small-group meeting of 2 to 10 RBTs, and the group can't go over 10 no matter how many supervisors attend.
  4. The supervisor observes the RBT with a client at least once a month. On-site observation is preferred, but video is allowed. Video monitoring alone, with no real-time interaction or feedback, may not count as supervision.
  5. Supervisors must be BCBA or BCaBA certified and must finish training based on the BACB's Supervisor Training Curriculum Outline (2.0) before they supervise. If the organization uses an RBT Requirements Coordinator, that person must be a BCBA.
  6. The requirements apply at each organization. An RBT who works for two organizations meets them at both. The handbook's example: 3% at one and 2% at the other doesn't count.
  7. Every client needs a supervisor. The RBT must have an RBT Supervisor or Requirements Coordinator for every client they work with, and that person must list the RBT as a supervisee in their BACB account.
  8. Supervision is client focused and separate from professional development. One event can't count for both.
  9. Records are kept for at least 7 years: the days and times the RBT provided services, the dates and length of supervision, the format (individual or group), the dates of direct observations, the supervisors' names, proof of each supervisor's relationship to the client, and extra records such as session notes if anything doesn't match.

Tracking it each month in a spreadsheet

  1. Log every session with the date, RBT code, units or minutes and a status, then total completed service hours for each RBT for the calendar month.
  2. Log every supervision contact with the date, RBT, supervisor, minutes, individual or group, and whether it included observing the RBT with a client.
  3. For each RBT each month, check four things: supervision hours divided by service hours (at least 5%), at least two contacts, at least one individual contact, and at least one observation.
  4. Check partway through the month. The hours an RBT needs go up as their service hours go up, and in week two there's still time to schedule another contact.
  5. Keep the log and session records together so you can pull the documentation list above if the BACB asks. Setup details are in How do I track RBT supervision hours each month?

Source: BACB Registered Behavior Technician Handbook (updated October 2026), Ongoing Supervision and Supervision Documentation sections, read Nov 27, 2026. The BACB updates its requirements, and your employer or payer may require more. This is record-keeping guidance, not clinical or legal advice, so check the current handbook before relying on these numbers.

Common follow-up questions

Can RBT supervision be done in a group?

Partly. At least one of the two required monthly contacts has to be individual. The other can be a small-group meeting of 2 to 10 RBTs.

Does an RBT who isn't working need monthly supervision?

No. The handbook says monthly supervision isn't required while an RBT isn't providing behavior-analytic services, but the other maintenance requirements still apply. For a long break, the BACB suggests looking at voluntary inactive status.

What happens if an RBT doesn't get enough supervision?

The handbook says an RBT found substantially noncompliant faces termination of their certification, or of their eligibility to recertify, and can't reapply for six months. A Notice of Alleged Violation can also be filed against the supervisor or Requirements Coordinator. An RBT who finds they're short should report it to the BACB.
