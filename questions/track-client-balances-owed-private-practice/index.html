How do therapists keep track of what clients owe?

Log every session with what was charged and what has been paid so far. The difference is what's still owed for that session. Add those up by client and group them by how old the session is, for example 0 to 30 days, 31 to 60 days and over 60 days. Look at the older groups first, and follow up on a set day each month.

How to set it up

  1. Log each session the day it happens, with the date, a client ID code, the amount charged and how it was paid.
  2. Update the payment when money arrives, on the session it belongs to. For partial payments, enter the total received so far.
  3. Include missed-session fees with the rest of the balance, so a client's total is in one place.
  4. Group balances by session age. Unpaid amounts from last week and from three months ago need different follow-up.
  5. Pick an overdue point, such as 30 days, and review everything past it once a month. Send a statement or have a short conversation, following your written payment policy.
  6. Keep insurance money in view separately. A session waiting on an insurance payment isn't the same as a client copay that's late, so note which is which when you follow up.

What you can bill a client, when, and how you collect it depends on your payer contracts, your written payment policy and your state's rules. Check your payer contracts before billing a client for any amount insurance didn't pay.

Common follow-up questions

Should I let clients carry a balance?

That's a practice policy decision. Whatever you choose, putting it in writing at intake and tracking balances by age makes it easier to apply the same way to everyone.

How do I handle a client who pays part of a session?

Record the total paid so far on that session. The rest stays on the balance and keeps aging until it's paid or you decide to write it off.

Can a client's balance include no-show fees?

In our tracker it does: missed-session fees are added to the client's balance and also listed in a separate fee ledger. Whether you charge them is up to your written policy and any payer rules.
